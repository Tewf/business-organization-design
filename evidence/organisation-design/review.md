# Review: five strands

Step 2 of the review, read on 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12 (the forward pass, Puranam 2014 in the original). It groups by strand the 61 sources its findings rest on, and answers Q1 to Q4 of [frame](frame.md), surveys first in each strand. The other three of the 64 (Doty 1993, Lee 2021 and the TOGAF page) serve the case against, the state of the art and the frame.

**Shape:** [review](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/39d3b25cd822a20277ee39c37b9e0130439802f2/shapes/review.md)

- Each key is in the [bibliography](../bibliography.md).
- Grades are the standard's four.
- A figure read in an abstract is marked (secondary); a work located only gives no figure.

## Strand 0: existing reviews

- Most economic transactions take place inside managed organisations, not in markets; a handbook surveys the theories, evidence and methods of organizational economics: structures, processes, boundaries and contracts (Gibbons 2012, publisher's blurb). Convention.
- The behavioural theory of the firm set an agenda that is traced through cognition, performance feedback, politics, attention, learning and adaptation (Gavetti 2012, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Management practices and decentralisation vary widely, more within countries than between them; larger firms and multinationals are better managed and more decentralised, and competition fosters both (Bloom 2010, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Organisations learn by encoding inferences from history into routines that guide behaviour: learning is routine-based, history-dependent and target-oriented (Levitt 1988, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Exploration and exploitation have become a lens for learning, design and alliances, with open debates on their definition and measurement (Lavie 2010, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Knowledge-based hierarchies have been used to study wage inequality, firm growth and offshoring, and the first empirical work confirms their main implications (Garicano 2015, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Agency theory is reviewed as empirically valid, best coupled with other perspectives (Eisenhardt 1989, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- No review joining the list of what a firm must do, the problems of its structure, its description as information processing and its adaptation was found (protocol, section 3). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 1: what a company must do (Q1)

- The word firm, as economics uses it, needs a definition apart from the plain man's use of it; the paper sets out to give one (Coase 1937, opening read). Convention.
- Cooperative specialisation raises productivity, and a theory of economic organisation must say when its gains are better obtained inside a firm than across markets (Alchian 1972, abstract of a reprint, secondary). Convention.
- Economic organisation is shaped by economising on transaction costs, under bounded rationality and opportunism; contracts are governed mainly by private ordering (Williamson 1985, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- The law gives a set of standard-form legal entities. Their essential role, beyond default contract terms, is to partition assets: the firm holds assets and makes contracts as a person distinct from its owners (Hansmann 2000, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Offered an intermediate form, the private limited liability company, most firms in four countries chose it over the corporation; where it came late, the partnership was used longer (Guinnane 2007, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Entity shielding, which protects the firm's assets from the owners' personal creditors, is economically and historically more significant than limited liability (Hansmann 2006, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- A firm can be disaggregated into activities, the discrete functions or processes of its value chain (Porter 1985, publisher's blurb). Convention.
- A process handbook organises examples of how organisations perform similar processes at several levels of abstraction, by inheritance and by the dependencies between activities (Malone 1999, abstract, secondary). Its online library holds over 5000 business activities (MIT 2003 Handbook, page read). Convention.
- A cross-industry process classification served to build a 24-item instrument of alignment between IT and business processes, used in interviews with managers of 66 small firms (Cragg 2007, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Process classification frameworks are treated in a handbook chapter of business process management (Aitken 2010, located only). Convention.
- An enterprise ontology defines the terms relevant to business enterprises, as a basis for enterprise modelling (Uschold 1998, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Business process management rests on the process model, which may configure an information system or be used to analyse and improve a process (van der Aalst 2013, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Firms are organisations that represent social knowledge of coordination and learning (Kogut 1996, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- A list of what every firm must do, derived from theory and tested for completeness, was not found (strings F5, F7, F44, F45). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 2: roles and human architecture (Q2)

- Any form of organisation must solve four universal problems: task division, task allocation, reward provision and information provision. The four split the division of labour (the first two) from the integration of effort (the last two), and each is a mapping: goals to tasks, tasks to agents, rewards to agents, information to agents. Solutions to the four are claimed individually necessary and collectively sufficient for an organisation to exist (Puranam 2014, pp. 165-166, read in the original). Convention.
- The four problems split into six: task division, task allocation, rewarding desired behaviour, eliminating free-riding, providing direction and ensuring coordination. Bureaucracy, adhocracy and self-managing organisations solve each differently (Martela 2019, full text, first half). Convention.
- Organisation design can abstract from the variety of organisations to a few universal problems of organizing and a few reusable building blocks (Puranam 2018, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Structure is the sum of the ways an organisation divides its labour into tasks and coordinates them. A synthesis of the research gives five parts (operating core, strategic apex, middle line, technostructure, support staff) and five configurations, from the simple structure to the adhocracy (Mintzberg 1979, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Propositions on design, technology and structure, for open and closed systems, are set out as an inventory for comparing organisations (Thompson 1967, publisher's blurb). Convention.
- Ownership and control are separated in the large corporation, and the separation is the paper's subject (Fama 1983, located only, title). Convention.
- The agency cost of separating ownership from control is the theory of the firm's ownership structure (Jensen 1976, located only). Convention.
- Corporate governance rests on the legal protection of investors and on ownership concentration (Shleifer 1997, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Boards' makeup and actions are jointly endogenous, which complicates every study of what boards do (Adams 2010, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Most firms are neither fully centralised nor fully decentralised. A high value of local adaptation goes with delegation and a high value of coordination with centralisation, as team theory predicts (McElheran 2014, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- The executive team of large US firms doubled in size from the mid-1980s, mainly through functional managers. Product and administrative functions changed differently, which the authors trace to the information each processes (Guadalupe 2013, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Algorithms direct, evaluate and discipline workers through six mechanisms: restricting, recommending, recording, rating, replacing and rewarding; workers resist them (Kellogg 2019, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Artificial intelligence may augment and replace the tasks of idea production, selling and scaling in a new venture, with implications for its organisational design (Chalmers 2020, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Over 239 effect sizes from 65 studies, bundles of human-resource practices had larger effects on business outcomes than single practices (Subramony 2009, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Practice writes who decides what as a role, apart from the person who fills it. A role has a purpose, domains it controls and accountabilities (HolacracyOne 2026 Constitution, page read; an example of practice only). Convention.

## Strand 3: the organisation as an information-processing system (Q3)

- Task uncertainty is related to organisational form through the cognitive limits of decision makers. Design strategies follow from those limits, and information systems and group problem solving are two of them (Galbraith 1974, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Uncertainty and information processing integrate the design literatures, and a contingency approach selects from a feasible set of structures (Tushman 1978, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Organisations are "systems of coordinated action" (March 1958, in its second edition, quoted in Martela 2019, secondary). Convention.
- Team theory is the seminal economic theory of a team's decisions (Marschak 1972, located only); its predictions on delegation are tested in Strand 2 (McElheran 2014). Convention.
- Managers are processors of limited capacity; an organisation is efficient by two measures, the number of processors and the delay between information and decision (Radner 1993, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a model.
- In real-time decentralised processing, decentralisation does not always reduce delay, because processing a message precludes processing current data (Van Zandt 1999, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a model.
- A knowledge-based hierarchy is a natural organisation when matching problems with those who can solve them is costly: workers learn the common problems and specialists the rare ones (Garicano 2000, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a model.
- Information technology and communication technology act in opposite directions, in firms of the US and seven European countries. Better information tools went with more autonomy and wider spans of control; better communication networks went with less autonomy (Bloom 2014, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Coordination is the management of dependencies among activities; each kind of dependency has its coordination processes (Malone 1994, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- The architecture of complex systems is the subject of the paper that the later work on decomposition and modularity starts from (Simon 1962, located only). Convention.
- A computational model operationalises Galbraith's view, simulating the information processing of a project organisation and predicting its performance (Levitt 1999, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- A design structure matrix represents a system's decomposition, including a team-based form for organisation design (Browning 2001, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Products, processes, contracts and firms are all designed; modularity is a theory of that design (Baldwin 2000, publisher's blurb). Convention.
- A standard notation gives a process model a machine-readable metamodel (OMG 2013 BPMN, page read). A modelling language describes processes, structures, information flows and systems in one language (OpenGroup 2026 ArchiMate, page read). Convention.
- A 6 x 6 matrix of interrogatives and transformations is claimed to hold every description of an enterprise (Zachman 2026 Framework, page read; an example of practice only). Convention.

## Strand 4: adaptation and learning (Q4)

- Adaptive processes refine exploitation faster than exploration, which makes them effective in the short run and self-destructive in the long run (March 1991, abstract, secondary). Measured, a simulation.
- When the effects of an organisation's attributes interact, its fitness landscape is rugged, and its form at founding persists, since the peak it finds depends on where it starts (Levinthal 1997, abstract, secondary). Measured, a simulation.
- A firm is described by its goals, its expectations and its choices, each a chapter of the theory (Cyert 1963, contents read). Convention.
- Low performance raises research spending through problemistic search and slack raises it through slack search, in shipbuilding firms (Greve 2003, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Skills and organisational capabilities found an evolutionary theory in which firms change by search and selection (Nelson 1982, contents read). Convention.
- A firm's advantage rests on distinctive processes of coordinating and combining, shaped by its asset positions and its path (Teece 1997, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- In 206 manufacturing firms, the interaction of exploration and exploitation was positively related to sales growth (He 2004, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Moderate linking between semi-isolated subgroups gave the highest equilibrium performance (Fang 2009, abstract, secondary). Measured, a simulation.
- Sequences of structures achieved higher performance than any fixed structure, in a simulation of five structures (Siggelkow 2005, abstract, secondary). Measured, a simulation.
- A rule for when a young firm should change its design, tested on firms, was not found (strings F25, F27, F33). Not found, in these searches.
