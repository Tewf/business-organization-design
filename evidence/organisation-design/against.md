# Against

The evidence against what the front page assumes, and the disagreements. Sources are in [review](review.md). None is deleted to make the subject look stronger.

**Shape:** [against](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/39d3b25cd822a20277ee39c37b9e0130439802f2/shapes/against.md)

## Against "a company's needs form a list a program can count"

- No list of what every firm must do, derived from theory and tested for completeness, was found (review, Strand 1). Not found, in these searches.
- The theory of the firm defines the firm by its boundary and its contracts, not by a list of functions (Coase 1937, Williamson 1985). Convention.
- A firm's advantage rests on processes distinctive to it and on its path, which a generic list does not hold (Teece 1997, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- The most adopted list is a practice taxonomy whose own page was not read here (protocol, G7). Its use in 66 small firms shows it can be applied, not that it is complete (Cragg 2007, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.

## Against "four problems describe the human architecture"

- The four problems are claimed sufficient for an organisation to exist, not to endure: adaptation to the environment is a further problem outside them (Martela 2019, full text, reading Puranam 2014). Convention.
- The best-known description of structure, Mintzberg's five ideal types, did not predict effectiveness in a formal test, while Miles and Snow's types predicted 24% of its variance (Doty 1993, abstract, secondary). Measured.

## Against "an organisation can be written as an information-processing system"

- The models disagree on a basic effect. The batch model characterises efficient networks and derives what follows for decentralisation (Radner 1993, abstract, secondary); in real time, decentralisation does not unambiguously reduce delay (Van Zandt 1999, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a model.
- Information-processing models leave out the incentives that agency theory holds (Eisenhardt 1989, abstract, secondary, recommending that agency be coupled with other perspectives). Supported with caveat.
- The practice that claims to hold every description of an enterprise gives no data for the claim (Zachman 2026 Framework, an example of practice only). Convention.

## Against "a young venture needs a designed structure"

- A study reconsiders the structure of start-ups and calls their flatness a myth (Lee 2021, located only, title). Convention, by title only.
- The field's evidence on structure and delegation comes from established firms: multi-establishment firms, 206 manufacturers and shipbuilders (McElheran 2014, He 2004, Greve 2003). Measured, in a different population.

## Disagreements

| Point | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Does size go with decentralisation? | larger firms and multinationals are more decentralised (Bloom 2010, abstract, secondary) | delegation is negatively correlated with firm size (McElheran 2014, abstract, secondary) |
| Does fit with one configuration make a firm effective? | five configurations are the elements of structure (Mintzberg 1979, abstract, secondary) | the five ideal types did not predict effectiveness (Doty 1993, abstract, secondary) |
| Is one structure the goal? | a contingency approach chooses the structure that fits the information to process (Tushman 1978, abstract, secondary) | a sequence of structures beats any fixed one (Siggelkow 2005, abstract, secondary) |

- A possible reason for the first split: Bloom reviews measures of decentralisation across firms and countries, McElheran measures one decision, IT purchasing, across establishments; the two may measure different rights.
- A possible reason for the third: the fit models compare structures at equilibrium, and the simulation compares paths of search.

## Against the sources themselves

- 43 of the 58 formal sources were read from their abstracts and 7 were located only; six books were read from their contents or a publisher's blurb. Most figures are marked (secondary).
- Four of the key results are simulations or models (March 1991, Levinthal 1997, Siggelkow 2005, Fang 2009), and three more are models (Radner 1993, Van Zandt 1999, Garicano 2000).
- The four universal problems are argued, not tested: the original states them as a definition and a claim of sufficiency (Puranam 2014, pp. 165-166).
- The grey sources are standards bodies and practice sellers on their own methods; two are below the threshold.
- OpenAlex sorts by citations, which favours old work, books with many reviews and American management journals.
