# Protocol: how the pool was built

A first pass, run on 2026-11-11 by one reviewer, with a second rater from another model family on the selection and the grey scores, and a forward pass on 2026-11-12 (section 12). Terms are in [frame](frame.md).

**Shape:** [protocol](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/39d3b25cd822a20277ee39c37b9e0130439802f2/shapes/protocol.md)

## 1. Why grey literature

Garousi's seven questions: one yes suggests including grey sources (Garousi 2019, Table 4).

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a lack of volume or quality in the formal literature? | no for the theory; yes for the lists of processes a company runs, which practice keeps as frameworks no journal reviews |
| Is the context important in the outcome? | yes: a structure that fits one environment fails in another, the claim of contingency theory |
| Is the goal to corroborate science with practice? | yes: whether a process framework covers what the theory says a firm must do |
| Is the goal to challenge assumptions of practice? | yes: whether a flat, role-based organisation solves the problems a hierarchy solves |
| Do practitioners provide most of the information? | yes, for process frameworks and modelling languages |
| Does the research focus on emerging practice? | yes: self-managing organisations |
| Is the subject a technology or a tool? | yes: modelling languages and notations |

## 2. Questions

- Q1 what must a company do, as an enumerable list of functions or processes, economic, legal, technical and human?
- Q2 what problems must its human architecture solve, and who decides what?
- Q3 how is an organisation written as an information-processing system, so that a program can read it?
- Q4 how does an organisation adapt and learn, and change its own design?

## 3. Existing reviews

- Found: a handbook of organizational economics (Gibbons 2012) and an assessment of the behavioural theory of the firm (Gavetti 2012).
- Found: surveys of the empirics of organisation (Bloom 2010), of exploration and exploitation (Lavie 2010) and of organisational learning (Levitt 1988).
- Found: surveys of knowledge-based hierarchies (Garicano 2015), of agency theory (Eisenhardt 1989) and of corporate governance (Shleifer 1997).
- Not found: a review that joins the list of what a firm must do, the problems its structure must solve, its description as information processing and its adaptation, for one firm.
- The business model's blocks, cash, demand, prices and markets are held by the neighbours named on the front page; this review does not search them.

## 6. Stopping rule

- Stated before the search, in the work's notes: the top 8 results per string by citations (N = 8), and a string stops after 5 consecutive results that add no concept (k = 5).
- Stated with it: the pool stops when a question's last three strings add no concept, or at 40 strings, whichever comes first.
- Applied: the per-string rule was applied on every row. No question reached saturation, so the cap decided. It was passed: F41 to F45 were run after the fortieth string, to reach Thompson's types of interdependence, differentiation and integration, modularity and the process classification. The cap is recorded as broken, not as met.
- The grey pages were chosen by the reviewer from the practice named in [frame](frame.md), one page each, with no ranking by an engine.

## 7. Selection

- Include: a work that defines, measures or models what a firm must do, as functions or processes.
- Include: a work on how its work is divided, allocated, rewarded, informed and governed, on how an organisation is represented formally, or on how it adapts and learns.
- Exclude: an application to one industry or one good with no method content; a work on markets, competition, prices, demand, cash or funding, held by the neighbours; a duplicate.
- Quality: a grey source scoring under 10 of 20 is an example of practice only.

## 8. Quality of grey sources

Garousi's 20 criteria in eight groups: authority 4, methodology 6, objectivity 4, date 1, position 1, novelty 2, impact 1, outlet 1. The threshold is 10 of 20. Scores are in [sources](sources.md).

## 9. Extraction and synthesis

- An abstract was read for each formal source with a figure; a figure from an abstract is marked (secondary) in [review](review.md).
- A book indexed with its contents or its publisher's blurb is marked so, and gives no figure.
- A work whose abstract the index did not hold is located only, and no figure is taken from it.
- One article was read in full text in its first half (Martela 2019). Puranam 2014 was first read through its quotations, then in the original, pages 165-166, on 12 Nov. 2026.
- Synthesis is narrative by question, traceable through [sources](sources.md).

## 10. Not searched, and limits

- Not searched: databases other than OpenAlex; Google Scholar; the company law of any country; labour law; the sociology of professions; organisational culture beyond one survey title; family firms.
- Not searched: a forward pass on the seminal works other than the four B1 rests on (section 12).
- Limits: one reviewer; the New concept letters are the reviewer's own call; OpenAlex returns by citations, which favours old work and books with many reviews.
- A page that answered an error was not read, and its absence says nothing about the practice it holds.

## 11. Second rater

- The second rater is a language model from another family than the first reviewer's.
- It was given the selection criteria of section 7 with the titles alone, and the criteria of section 8 with a description of each grey page.
- Selection: 24 titles, 17 included by the reviewer and 7 excluded. The two agreed on 23 of 24 (96%), Cohen's kappa 0.90.
- The one disagreement was the meta-analysis of human-resource bundles (Subramony 2009), excluded by the rater. A reread of the criterion settled it: how work is rewarded is in it, so the work stays.
- Grey scores: the two agreed on the side of the threshold for all 6 pages. The mean absolute difference of the totals was 2.8 points of 20; the rater scored the two pages below the threshold lower still (4 and 4 against 8 and 8).
